EvaCun2025

(click to read more)

The first project I got involved in was the EvaCun2025 Shared Task on Lemmatization of Ancient Cuneiform Languages, a workshop associated with the Ancient Language Processing sector of the NAACL conference. I worked with two colleagues on this task, Brandi Wilkins and John Sbur, under the guidance of Dr. Yudong Liu. This task involved using ChatGPT 4.0-Mini to lemmatize ancient cuneiform languages, which we did through the use of data processing and prompt engineering. I worked on the implementation of data processing infrastructure, performed data analysis, and helped build our conceptual model based off of successes in previous research by Dr. Liu as well as our own ideas.

We submitted our paper to the conference, and were invited to attend in April of 2025. At this conference, we got a chance to present our work and connect with other academics to learn about their methodologies on the same project. We attended talks and events to learn about new research and publications in the fields of AI and NLP. This experience introduced me to the world of academic research, and also helped me inform my own views on hot topics in the CS industry, like AI and ML. Our source code can be found here.

DARLing Lab

(click to read more)

Since April 2025, I have been working as a member of the DARLing lab (Documentation, Analysis, and Revitalization), I work with the Hän language under the guidance of Maura O'Leary. We are working to preserve this endangered language and expand access to language-learning materials. I am part of the Transducer team, where we work on maintaining a morphological transducer that breaks a Hän word into its English counterpart and inflectional morphemes, or takes an English word and attaches the inflectional morphemes to form the proper Hän word. This maintenance involves the entry of data from a dictionary into the transducer through the use of lexd, as well as checking the format of existing and new entries to the transducer. We also analyze verb paradigms to add verb entries to the transducer, using different data from the charts to assess which morphological rules can apply to the given verb.

Since September 2025, I have worked as a lab manager with the DARLing lab, I have been helping onboard new members and prepare them for the research assistant role. I have also been working on documentation, making sure that we have guides and FAQ documents for various parts of the lab to help speed up the onboarding process.

KIND Lab

(click to read more)

In December 2025, I joined Dr. Yasmine Elglaly's KIND Lab, which focuses on accessibility in computing. The project I work on within the lab is using research-based strategies to develop an educational game called You&I for use by speech and language pathology practitioners with young children diagnosed with ASD, specifically children that experience pronoun reversal. I have worked with Tyanin Opdahl to finish a paper investigating prior research and presenting her previously conducted survey, which resulted in design recommendations from speech language pathology professionals and other professionals that work with young autistic individuals, such as Special Education teachers in elementary schools. Currently, I work with Tyanin and Ryan Critchlow to conduct a pilot study in which the first level of the implemented game is being tested with young children with ASD, which eventually will result in recommendations for our next steps, which will shape how future implementation of the game goes before the final result is published. I am working on this project as my Senior Research capstone for the CS Department with my colleagues Kayla Warner and Rizon Takabe. We will be helped rewrite the code and design and implement 2-3 new levels alongside several minigames. We will write a paper and present our findings, which will pertain to how this educational game can help young children with autism in their speech development, and what features are important for other educational games with this goal to implement.

SIGSE TS 2027

(click to read more)

Tyanin and our instructor, Yasmine Elglaly, obtained a grant alongside Bedour Alshaigy to work on this project, which involves studying the ways that neurodivergent students interact with generative AI for academic purposes. The three of them designed a study to investigate this relationship, which involved having neurodivergent introductory CS students perform a recursion-based coding task. The students were given the option to use ChatGPT, which was set up in another window logged into a blank account created by the researcher. This study was run by Tyanin and Yasmine at our university, but the sister study run by Bedour at Uppsala University was unable to be completed.

They compiled the results of that study into a set of materials which became the basis for the second part of the project -- a focus group of instructors. This focus group would be asked to read through the materials provided (a record of the chatlog interspersed with updated screenshots of the participant's code) and annotate with observations and opinions. They were asked to give their ideas on how ChatGPT was a benefit or detriment for the user, how helpful were ChatGPT's responses, and how would they have responded differently as an instructor, all with particular consideration for the role neurodivergence plays in the interaction. Then, the group was asked to discuss their thoughts and opinions. Finally, they were asked to design a prototype tool that would improve ChatGPT's effectiveness as a tutor. Sophia Sloane and I helped run this focus group, synthesize and analyze the findings, and write the part of the paper about the focus group. This paper has been submitted to the SIGSE TS 2027 committee for review.

MedAI

(click to read more)

Dr. Moushumi Sharmin started this project as a response to her previous work, which investigated the ability of sex workers in Bangladesh to access healthcare. The goal of the MedAI project is to create a pipeline that connects an app to an AI chatbot, connecting volunteer doctors to these workers with the AI chatbot acting as a tool to help reduce the load on either party. The app provides a simple interface for 'patient' and doctor users, which can accept audio and visual input. This information is translated from Bengali/Bangla to English, then passed to an AI chatbot API. This chatbot analyzes image data, summarizes the translated transcript of the audio data, and proposes diagnoses. This is immediately sent to a volunteer doctor, who is asked to review the summary and analysis to ensure that nothing is missed and that appropriate medical advice is conveyed to the patient. The patient and doctor are put in contact to facilitate follow-up questions on either end. The doctor's feedback is sent back to the patient for review. This project is currently in its beginning stages.

I have been working within the image detection subgroup, where we are working on a literature review to handle the inequital results of image detection in dermatological models pertaining to skin color and the model's ability to properly interpret and diagnose skin conditions. We are working with the previous work of Aayushman et al, which proposes a strategy for reducing this inequity to make a more effective medical tool. This approach will shape our image detection and help the pipeline more accurately convey the patient's condition to the doctor.

Thoughts on GenAI

Despite becoming involved with many projects that involve generative AI, I have complicated feelings about AI and tend to be against the use of generative AI. Part of my concern comes from the societal inequity inherent to this field due to social bias encoded in web-scraped training data, privacy concerns, social inequalities in accessing generative AI (e.g. most genAI tools being primarily in English), and the inequitable climate impact. The climate impact itself is a major concern, as data centers use large amounts of both water for cooling and energy. However, I recognize that AI is a powerful tool and can be used to do or elevate good work being done, and that other forms of computing also have similar social and environmental impacts. Overall, I have developed the position that AI should be used very cautiously with thorough consideration of its various impacts, and should not be used for trivial tasks or tasks that do not have an inherent social benefit. (For example, I would not repeat my participation in the EvaCun2025 shared task due to its reliance on genAI, though I learned a lot and found it very rewarding).
